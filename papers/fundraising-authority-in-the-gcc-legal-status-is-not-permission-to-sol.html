Fundraising Authority in the GCC: Legal Status Is Not Permission to Solicit

Incorporation, nonprofit status, public fundraising, receipt of foreign donations, and outward transfers are separate legal questions. A campaign authorization record connects the entity, regulator, permit, approved purpose, channels, account, geography, dates, and reporting duties before any public solicitation begins.

The category error

Registration as a nonprofit or foundation does not by itself establish a general right to solicit donations. In the GCC laws examined here, legal personality and fundraising authority are distinct questions. The UAE's federal donations law regulates who may collect donations, through which channels, and under what permissions, including activity in free zones. Its public-welfare law governs recognized organizations but does not erase the separate donation regime (United Arab Emirates, 2021, 2023).

The same logic applies to cross-border activity. An entity may lawfully operate domestically yet need a separate approval to receive money from abroad or send funds to an overseas partner. Qatar's regulatory architecture places charitable fundraising and international activity under specialized supervision. A partnership agreement cannot substitute for regulator authorization (Regulatory Authority for Charitable Activities, 2025).

A campaign authorization memo

Before public communication begins, the campaign owner should prepare a short authorization memo. It identifies the exact legal entity asking for funds, the licensed partner if different, the approving authority, permit number and validity, approved channels, receiving bank account, purpose, geography, beneficiary group, use of unspent funds, and any cross-border approvals. Marketing, finance, legal, and program teams should sign off against the same document.

This memo converts compliance from a late legal check into a design input. If a social media channel is not covered, the campaign does not use it. If the permit applies only to a defined period or purpose, the landing page and payment process reflect those limits. If funds must pass through a licensed charity, contracts and donor receipts should make that role transparent.

Institutional controls

A permit register should track application date, authority, purpose, approved amount, channels, bank account, start and end dates, reporting deadline, and closure status. Digital systems should prevent a campaign from going live without a valid record. Finance should reconcile receipts to the approved campaign and flag excess, late, foreign, or misdirected payments. Compliance should document any regulator communication and decisions about refunds or reallocation.

The legal analysis must be refreshed when the operating model changes. Moving from institutional grants to public crowdfunding, adding a foreign donor, accepting crypto-assets, launching an app, or transferring delivery to a downstream partner can create new permissions and risk. The proper question is not whether the organization was approved once, but whether this activity, channel, source, destination, and purpose are approved now.

Jurisdictional evidence and limits

Primary law confirms the central distinction. UAE Federal Law No. 3 of 2021 defines licensed parties separately from entities authorized by a fundraising permit. Qatar's Law No. 15 of 2014, as amended, requires authorization to collect, receive, or disburse donations for a specified purpose and period. Bahrain's Decree-Law No. 21 of 2013 prohibits collection for public purposes without a license, and Saudi Arabia publishes a distinct donation-collection law through the NCNP (Kingdom of Bahrain, 2013; National Center for Non-Profit Sector, 2026; State of Qatar, 2014; United Arab Emirates, 2021).

The verified rule is not that every GCC jurisdiction uses the same permit. It is that incorporation, public-benefit status, fundraising, receipt of foreign donations, and outward transfer are separate legal questions whose answers depend on jurisdiction, actor, channel, purpose, and date. The campaign authorization memo operationalizes that distinction without replacing local legal advice.

Tracing one campaign end to end

A useful control test follows one campaign from board approval to final public report. Review the legal entity, permit, copy, channels, payment route, receipt, ledger, transfer, beneficiary purpose, and closure report. The test should confirm that every public statement can be traced to an authorization and every financial movement to an approved account and purpose. Any break in the chain becomes a corrective action with an owner and deadline.

Conclusion

Primary law in the reviewed jurisdictions confirms that establishment and fundraising authority are separate questions. The relevant compliance analysis must identify the actor, purpose, channel, account, geography, permit, and reporting duties for the specific campaign. A campaign authorization memo records that analysis but does not replace current jurisdiction-specific advice.
