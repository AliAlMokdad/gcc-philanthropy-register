The UAE's Public-Benefit Organization Framework: Associations, National Societies, Unions, and Solidarity Funds

Federal Decree-Law No. 50 of 2023 defines four public-benefit forms and assigns licensing to the competent federal or local authority. The forms differ in founders, membership, allocated property, and collective purpose, so legal classification should precede registration and fundraising planning.

Four statutory forms

An association is a group of natural persons formed to conduct one or more public-welfare activities without aiming for profit. A national society is created by one or more natural-person founders through allocation of money for public-welfare activity. A union brings together at least five national societies or associations conducting similar activities. A social solidarity fund is created by legal persons to promote solidarity among affiliated members (United Arab Emirates, 2023).

The forms organize different constituencies. Associations are membership bodies. National societies are founder-and-asset bodies. Unions coordinate eligible organizations. Solidarity funds serve affiliated members. A founder should test the proposed constituency and asset model rather than choosing the most familiar English label.

Federal law and local competence

The law defines the competent authority as the local authority responsible for licensing and supervising public-benefit organizations, or the federal ministry where no local competent authority exists. This design creates a national framework with emirate-level implementation where local authorities operate (United Arab Emirates, 2023, 2025).

The Ministry of Community Empowerment maintains national nonprofit-sector services and a register of licensed associations. In Dubai, the Community Development Authority regulates nonprofit civil organizations, while charitable institutions and activities also engage IACAD's mandate. The correct path depends on form, activity, and emirate (Community Development Authority, n.d.; Ministry of Community Empowerment, n.d.).

Governance follows the form

Constitutional documents should address purposes, membership or founders, governance, representation, financial resources, conflicts, records, amendments, branches, dissolution, and remaining assets in the manner required for the chosen form. A union or solidarity fund cannot be drafted as if it were an ordinary association because the participating legal persons and internal rights differ.

Public-benefit status also does not answer every donation question. Federal donation law, emirate procedures, campaign permits, and banking controls remain relevant to collection and transfer.

Current-law boundary

Federal Decree-Law No. 50 took effect within a framework supplemented by Cabinet Resolution No. 5 of 2025. The official English text notes that Arabic controls interpretation in case of conflict. Applications should therefore use current Arabic legal and service materials where precise interpretation is material (United Arab Emirates, 2023, 2025).

A free-zone company, family foundation, trust, or ordinary commercial company is not automatically one of the four public-benefit forms.

The UAE form-classification test

Checking the form against the operating model

Model the first two years of governance and transactions. Show who joins, appoints, votes, controls assets, receives funds, contracts, opens branches, and approves dissolution. Any function unsupported by the chosen form requires redesign or a different legal route.

Conclusion

The UAE framework offers distinct forms for collective membership, founder-allocated assets, organizational coordination, and member solidarity. Accurate classification improves both licensing and later regulatory compliance.
